\begin{abstract}
We give a finite deterministic model separating (i) reachability in a fixed generative system,
(ii) capability identity modulo transformations already present in that system, and
(iii) verified changes of the generative system itself. The model exhibits two-generation
verifier-dependent discoverability, exact closure obstruction, quotient transport, causal
ablation, scoped applicability and revocation. It also falsifies a stronger statement:
the second capability is already syntactically formable in the raw constructor meta-language
before the first capability is acquired. Thus the example establishes developmental
admissibility/reachability, not strict second-order growth of constructor syntax.
\end{abstract}

\section{Old system}

Let
\[
\Sigma=\{\mathrm{LT},\mathrm{LE},\mathrm{AND},\mathrm{OR},A,B,C,D\},
\qquad X=\Sigma^4.
\]
Let $G=C_4=\langle \rho\mid \rho^4=e\rangle$ act on $X$ by cyclic permutation of coordinates.

\begin{definition}
The old closure of $x\in X$ is
\[
\Cl_0(x)=G\cdot x.
\]
\end{definition}

Every element of $G$ preserves the multiset of tokens.

\begin{proposition}[Old-closure obstruction]
For
\[
x_1=(\mathrm{LT},A,B,C),\quad t_1=(\mathrm{LE},A,B,C)
\]
and
\[
x_2=(A,B,\mathrm{LT},C),\quad t_2=(A,B,\mathrm{LE},C),
\]
one has $t_i\notin\Cl_0(x_i)$ for $i=1,2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The $G$-orbit preserves token multiset, whereas each target replaces one occurrence of
$\mathrm{LT}$ by $\mathrm{LE}$.
\end{proof}

\section{Constructor language and capability identity}

For $i\in\{0,1,2,3\}$ and $a\neq b\in\Sigma$, let
\[
r_{i,a\to b}:X\rightharpoonup X
\]
be the partial map replacing $a$ by $b$ at coordinate $i$, defined when the $i$th coordinate is $a$.
The frozen one-step constructor language contains all such maps.

For a source/target pair $(x,t)$ define the verifier
\[
V_{x,t}(r)=1\iff r(x)=t.
\]
The unique literal survivors for the two source cases are respectively
\[
r_{0,\mathrm{LT}\to\mathrm{LE}}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
r_{2,\mathrm{LT}\to\mathrm{LE}}.
\]
Their literal intersection is empty.

\begin{definition}[Closure-relative capability identity]
For one-site rewrites define
\[
r\sim_0 r'
\iff
\exists g\in G\; r'=g r g^{-1},
\]
where equality is equality of partial maps on $X$. A closure-relative capability is an orbit
$[r]_0$ under this action.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Quotient identity]
The two source cases determine the same unique capability class
\[
O_1=[\mathrm{LT}\to\mathrm{LE}]_0.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The two literal survivors differ only by cyclic conjugation of the application coordinate.
\end{proof}

This quotient is also recovered by an independently implemented constructor grammar in which
one rewrites only slot $0$ and obtains other positions by conjugation with the old $C_4$ action.
Hence the two syntactic constructor presentations recover the same orbit class.

\section{Transport, scope, and revocation}

For the held-out pair
\[
h=(A,\mathrm{LT},B,C),\qquad h'=(A,\mathrm{LE},B,C),
\]
literal-identity transfer fails, while $O_1$ contains the required transported representative
$r_{1,\mathrm{LT}\to\mathrm{LE}}$. Removing $O_1$ restores failure.

Let the observable context set be
\[
C=\{\mathtt{if},\mathtt{json},\mathtt{loop},\mathtt{return}\}
\]
and freeze the scope grammar
\[
\mathcal S=\{\top\}\cup\{[c=c_0]\mid c_0\in C\}.
\]
Positive evidence requires $O_1$ on \texttt{if}; protected evidence forbids it on \texttt{json}.
The unique minimal surviving scope is
\[
s_1(c)=[c=\mathtt{if}].
\]
Later counterevidence appears inside this scope. No member of $\mathcal S$ then contains all
positive cases while excluding all harmful cases, so the lifecycle decision is revocation.

\section{Second generation}

Let
\[
d=(A,\mathrm{LT},B,\mathrm{AND}),\qquad
 d'=(A,\mathrm{LE},B,\mathrm{OR}).
\]

\begin{proposition}[Cold one-step obstruction]
No one-site rewrite $r$ satisfies $r(d)=d'$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$d$ and $d'$ differ in two coordinates, whereas a one-site rewrite changes at most one.
\end{proof}

The exhaustive constructor audit therefore has $0$ verifier survivors among the $28$ available
one-site candidates.

Applying the retained $O_1$ uses only the current state and the capability class; the target is
not available to the application procedure. In this case the source token occurs uniquely, so
one obtains
\[
d_1=(A,\mathrm{LE},B,\mathrm{AND}).
\]
The same one-step constructor audit now has one surviving capability class
\[
O_2=[\mathrm{AND}\to\mathrm{OR}]_1.
\]

\section{Transition categories}

Let $\mathcal C_0$ be the subcategory of $\Par(X)$ generated by the total cyclic transports.
Let
\[
\mathcal C_1=\langle\mathcal C_0,O_1\rangle
\]
be generated by the old transports and the transported representatives of $O_1$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact second-generation closure expansion]
\[
d'\notin\Reach_{\mathcal C_1}(d).
\]
After adjoining $O_2$,
\[
\mathcal C_2=\langle\mathcal C_1,O_2\rangle,
\qquad
d'\in\Reach_{\mathcal C_2}(d).
\]
Moreover, ablating either $O_1$ or $O_2$ restores failure.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Morphisms generated by $\mathcal C_0$ and $O_1$ can permute coordinates and replace
$\mathrm{LT}$ by $\mathrm{LE}$, but cannot create an $\mathrm{OR}$ token from $\mathrm{AND}$.
Thus $d'$ is outside the complete $\mathcal C_1$ reachability closure. Adjoining $O_2$ supplies
exactly the missing second transformation. Each single ablation leaves one of the two coordinate
differences unresolved.
\end{proof}

Cold exhaustive reconstruction by two literal rewrites considers $28^2=784$ ordered pairs;
after retaining $O_1$, the complete second-generation one-step audit considers $28$, a $28\times$
compression. This is an operational consequence, not the definition of development.

\section{The stronger statement fails}

Let $M_0$ be the raw frozen constructor meta-language containing all one-site rewrites.
Although $O_2$ has no cold verifier-surviving one-step use on $(d,d')$, the syntax
$r_{i,\mathrm{AND}\to\mathrm{OR}}$ is already present in $M_0$. Hence
\[
O_2\in\Form(M_0).
\]
Therefore the stronger second-order claim
\[
O_2\notin\Form(M_0)
\quad\text{and}\quad
O_2\in\Form(M_1)
\]
is false in this model.

The exact supported distinction is instead
\[
\operatorname{Survive}_1(d,d')=\varnothing,
\qquad
\operatorname{Survive}_1(d_1,d')=\{O_2\}.
\]
Thus $O_1$ changes verifier-relative developmental discoverability/admissibility, while it does
not enlarge the raw formation grammar.

\section{Categorical question}

The experiment suggests two levels that should not be identified:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{internal computation}: morphisms $x\to y$ inside a fixed continuation regime
$\mathcal C_t$;
\item \emph{developmental change}: a transformation of regimes
\[
\mathcal C_t\longrightarrow\mathcal C_{t+1}.
\]
\end{enumerate}

In the finite witness one has
\[
\mathcal C_0\hookrightarrow\mathcal C_1\hookrightarrow\mathcal C_2.
\]
A natural higher-level object would therefore have continuation regimes as objects and
verified admissions, restrictions or revocations as arrows. We do not claim that ordinary
categories are the final structure: the appropriate object may require action groupoids,
indexed/fibred categories, bicategories, profunctors/optics, or another construction.

\begin{question}
What is the natural categorical identity of a capability when literal implementations are
quotiented by transformations already internal to the current regime? Is the orbit quotient in
this finite model the correct prototype, or should identity be given by a universal/observational
construction such as a coequalizer, localization or bicategorical quotient?
\end{question}

\begin{question}
What structure best separates state evolution $x\to y$ inside $\mathcal C_t$ from verified
changes $\mathcal C_t\to\mathcal C_{t+1}$ of the continuation space itself?
\end{question}

\begin{question}
Should applicability be indexed over a context base, so that counterevidence restricts the
region over which a capability exists, and revocation corresponds to loss of every admissible
section/scope?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Strict second-order development]
What is the right compositional criterion for a transition of constructor regimes
$M_0\to M_1$ for which a later capability satisfies
\[
O_2\notin\Form(M_0),\qquad O_2\in\Form(M_1),
\]
without making the result tautological by hard-coding $O_1$ into the formation rule for $O_2$?
\end{question}
