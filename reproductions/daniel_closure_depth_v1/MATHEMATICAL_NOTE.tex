\begin{abstract}
We give a finite deterministic model separating (i) reachability in a fixed generative regime,
(ii) capability identity modulo transformations already present in that regime, and
(iii) verified changes of the regime itself. The model exhibits two-generation
verifier-dependent discoverability, exact closure obstruction, orbit transport, causal
ablation, scoped applicability and revocation. It also falsifies a stronger statement:
the second capability is already syntactically formable in the raw constructor meta-language
before the first capability is acquired. Thus the example establishes development of
verified reachability/admissibility, not strict second-order growth of constructor syntax.
\end{abstract}

\section{Old regime}

Let
\[
\Sigma=\{\mathrm{LT},\mathrm{LE},\mathrm{AND},\mathrm{OR},A,B,C,D\},
\qquad X=\Sigma^4.
\]
Let $G=C_4=\langle \rho\mid \rho^4=e\rangle$ act on $X$ by cyclic permutation of coordinates.

\begin{definition}
The old closure of $x\in X$ is
\[
\Cl_0(x)=G\cdot x.
\]
\end{definition}

Every element of $G$ preserves the multiset of tokens.

\begin{proposition}[Old-closure obstruction]
For
\[
x_1=(\mathrm{LT},A,B,C),\quad t_1=(\mathrm{LE},A,B,C)
\]
and
\[
x_2=(A,B,\mathrm{LT},C),\quad t_2=(A,B,\mathrm{LE},C),
\]
one has $t_i\notin\Cl_0(x_i)$ for $i=1,2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The $G$-orbit preserves token multiset, whereas each target replaces one occurrence of
$\mathrm{LT}$ by $\mathrm{LE}$.
\end{proof}

\section{Constructor language and capability identity}

For $i\in\{0,1,2,3\}$ and $a\neq b\in\Sigma$, let
\[
r_{i,a\to b}:X\rightharpoonup X
\]
be the partial map replacing $a$ by $b$ at coordinate $i$, defined when the $i$th coordinate is $a$.
The frozen one-step constructor language contains all such maps.

For a source/target pair $(x,t)$ define the external verifier
\[
V_{x,t}(r)=1\iff r(x)=t.
\]
The unique literal survivors for the two source cases are respectively
\[
r_{0,\mathrm{LT}\to\mathrm{LE}}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
r_{2,\mathrm{LT}\to\mathrm{LE}}.
\]
Their literal intersection is empty.

\begin{definition}[Closure-relative capability identity]
Let $G$ act on one-site partial rewrites by conjugation,
\[
g\cdot r = g r g^{-1}.
\]
Define
\[
r\sim_G r'
\iff
\exists g\in G\; r'=g r g^{-1}.
\]
A closure-relative capability class in this witness is the orbit $[r]_G$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Quotient identity]
The two source cases determine the same unique capability class
\[
O_1=[\mathrm{LT}\to\mathrm{LE}]_G.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The two literal survivors differ only by cyclic conjugation of the application coordinate.
\end{proof}

This orbit is also recovered by an independently implemented constructor grammar in which
one rewrites only slot $0$ and obtains other positions by conjugation with the old $C_4$ action,
\[
\rho^{-k};r_{0,a\to b};\rho^k.
\]
Thus two genuinely different constructor syntaxes recover the same capability class.

\section{Transport, scope, and revocation}

For the held-out pair
\[
h=(A,\mathrm{LT},B,C),\qquad h'=(A,\mathrm{LE},B,C),
\]
literal-identity transfer fails, while $O_1$ contains the required transported representative
$r_{1,\mathrm{LT}\to\mathrm{LE}}$. Removing $O_1$ restores failure.

Let the observable context set be
\[
C=\{\mathtt{if},\mathtt{json},\mathtt{loop},\mathtt{return}\}
\]
and freeze the scope grammar
\[
\mathcal S=\{\top\}\cup\{[c=c_0]\mid c_0\in C\}.
\]
Positive evidence requires $O_1$ on \texttt{if}; protected evidence forbids it on \texttt{json}.
The unique minimal surviving scope is
\[
s_1(c)=[c=\mathtt{if}].
\]
Later counterevidence appears inside this scope. No member of $\mathcal S$ then contains all
positive cases while excluding all harmful cases, so the lifecycle decision is revocation.

\section{Reachability categories}

To keep state transitions and operator syntax type-distinct, define a thin transition category
from a family of partial state transformations.

\begin{definition}
For a set $F$ of partial endomaps on $X$, let $\mathcal C(F)$ be the preorder-category whose
objects are states $x\in X$, with a unique arrow
\[
x\longrightarrow y
\]
iff some finite composable word in transformations from $F$ is defined at $x$ and sends $x$ to $y$.
\end{definition}

Let
\[
F_0=G,
\qquad
\mathcal C_0=\mathcal C(F_0),
\]
where elements of $G$ are regarded as total transformations of $X$. Let
\[
F_1=F_0\cup O_1,
\qquad
\mathcal C_1=\mathcal C(F_1),
\]
where $O_1$ denotes all literal representatives in its $G$-orbit.

\begin{remark}
The thin category forgets multiplicity of derivation/program paths and retains exactly the
reachability relation needed by the finite experiment. A richer semantics could retain those paths.
\end{remark}

\section{Second generation}

Let
\[
d=(A,\mathrm{LT},B,\mathrm{AND}),\qquad
 d'=(A,\mathrm{LE},B,\mathrm{OR}).
\]

\begin{proposition}[Cold one-step obstruction]
No one-site rewrite $r$ satisfies $r(d)=d'$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$d$ and $d'$ differ in two coordinates, whereas a one-site rewrite changes at most one.
\end{proof}

The exhaustive constructor audit therefore has zero verifier survivors among the $28$ available
one-site candidates.

Applying the retained $O_1$ uses only the current state and the capability class; the target is
not available to the application procedure. Since the source token occurs uniquely, one obtains
\[
d_1=(A,\mathrm{LE},B,\mathrm{AND}).
\]
The same one-step constructor audit now has one surviving orbit class
\[
O_2=[\mathrm{AND}\to\mathrm{OR}]_G.
\]
The quotient still uses the fixed old symmetry group $G$; what is relative to generation $1$ is
the closure/reachability test, not a newly asserted equivalence relation.

Let
\[
F_2=F_1\cup O_2,
\qquad
\mathcal C_2=\mathcal C(F_2).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Exact second-generation closure expansion]
There is no arrow $d\to d'$ in $\mathcal C_1$, whereas there is an arrow
$d\to d'$ in $\mathcal C_2$. Moreover, ablating either $O_1$ or $O_2$ restores failure.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Transformations in $F_1$ can cyclically permute coordinates and replace $\mathrm{LT}$ by
$\mathrm{LE}$, but cannot create an $\mathrm{OR}$ token from $\mathrm{AND}$. Thus $d'$ is
unreachable in $\mathcal C_1$. Adding $O_2$ supplies the missing second change. Either single
ablation leaves one required token change unavailable.
\end{proof}

Cold exhaustive reconstruction by two literal rewrites considers $28^2=784$ ordered pairs;
after retaining $O_1$, the complete second-generation one-step audit considers $28$, a $28\times$
compression. This is an operational consequence, not the definition of development.

\section{The stronger statement fails}

Let $M_0$ be the raw frozen constructor meta-language containing all one-site rewrites.
Although $O_2$ has no cold verifier-surviving one-step use on $(d,d')$, the syntax
$r_{i,\mathrm{AND}\to\mathrm{OR}}$ is already present in $M_0$. Hence
\[
O_2\in\Form(M_0).
\]
Therefore the stronger second-order claim
\[
O_2\notin\Form(M_0)
\quad\text{and}\quad
O_2\in\Form(M_1)
\]
is false in this model.

The exact supported distinction is instead
\[
\operatorname{Survive}_1(d,d')=\varnothing,
\qquad
\operatorname{Survive}_1(d_1,d')=\{O_2\}.
\]
Thus $O_1$ changes verifier-relative developmental discoverability/admissibility, while it does
not enlarge the raw formation grammar.

\section{Categorical question}

The experiment exposes two different levels.

\subsection*{Internal computation}
For fixed $t$, computation is represented by arrows
\[
x\to y\quad\text{in }\mathcal C_t.
\]
A search or policy selects continuations internal to that regime.

\subsection*{Developmental change}
The evidence also induces a sequence of different reachability categories
\[
\mathcal C_0,\qquad \mathcal C_1,\qquad \mathcal C_2.
\]
The developmental question concerns whatever higher-level structure should represent transitions
\[
\mathcal C_t\rightsquigarrow\mathcal C_{t+1}
\]
including admission, restriction and revocation. The symbol $\rightsquigarrow$ is deliberately
neutral: the experiment does not assume these regime changes are ordinary functors, inclusions,
optics, profunctors, $2$-cells, or something else.

\begin{question}
What is the natural categorical identity of a capability when literal implementations are
quotiented by transformations already internal to the current regime? Is the orbit quotient in
this finite model the correct prototype, or should identity be given by a universal/observational
construction such as an action groupoid, coequalizer, localization or bicategorical quotient?
\end{question}

\begin{question}
What categorical object should keep arrows $x\to y$ inside a fixed $\mathcal C_t$ distinct from
verified transitions $\mathcal C_t\rightsquigarrow\mathcal C_{t+1}$ of the continuation regime itself?
\end{question}

\begin{question}
Should applicability be indexed or fibred over a context base, so that counterevidence restricts
the region over which a capability is admissible and revocation occurs when no admissible region remains?
\end{question}

\section{Strict second-order development}

The stronger target requires constructor regimes $M_t$ as well as execution/reachability regimes
$\mathcal C_t$. A strict result would require an admitted $O_1$ that causally changes
\[
M_0\rightsquigarrow M_1
\]
and a later capability satisfying
\[
O_2\notin\Form(M_0),
\qquad
O_2\in\Form(M_1).
\]
The hard part is to establish this without making it tautological by defining the $O_2$
constructor to take $O_1$ as a hard-coded argument.

\begin{question}
What is the right compositional notion of second-order development that distinguishes changed
reachability, changed admissibility, changed representation, and changed constructor formability?
\end{question}
