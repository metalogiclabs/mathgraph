# Closure-relative capability identity and strict reachability growth

This Lean project formalizes the exact finite mathematical witness in MathGraph's research note **Closure-relative capability growth: exact finite witness and categorical question**. The witness was specified and executed before this Palomar package; the Lean definitions mirror that finite model rather than being chosen after the fact to force the advertised conclusions.

## Mathematical question

Adaptive search systems often use the word *capability* for several different objects. This witness separates four of them:

1. a literal rewrite program;
2. a rewrite class modulo transformations already available in the old regime;
3. reachability under the currently retained transition regime;
4. syntactic formability in the raw constructor language.

Let the state space be four token coordinates with the cyclic group `C₄` acting by coordinate transport. A one-site rewrite is identified with another when the old cyclic symmetry transports its coordinate while leaving its source and destination tokens unchanged. This is the finite orbit model of **closure-relative capability identity**.

Two coordinate-distinct `LT → LE` rewrites lie in one such orbit. Retaining that orbit enlarges the available transition regime, but it cannot produce an `OR` token. For the protected source

`(A, LT, B, AND)`

and target

`(A, LE, B, OR)`,

the target is therefore unreachable in regime 1. Regime 2 adjoins the transported `AND → OR` class, after which the target is reachable by reusing `LT → LE` and then applying `AND → OR`.

The final theorem records an important negative boundary: `AND → OR` was already a literal member of the raw one-site constructor language. Thus this witness proves **strict growth of verified reachability**, not growth of what the host syntax can express.

## Why this is a research object

The finite theorem is a deliberately small separator for questions in automated reasoning and adaptive verified search. It shows, in one exact model, that literal identity, quotient-level capability identity, regime-relative reachability, and raw syntactic expressibility are different mathematical notions. The source note uses this separation to pose the broader structural question of what should replace a literal symmetry orbit in systems where capability identity is observational or future-relative rather than generated by a finite group action.

The formalization does **not** claim that this elementary finite witness by itself settles that broader problem. It supplies a machine-checkable base case and a precise falsification boundary for a larger research programme.

## Claim boundary

The compared declarations prove only:

- orbit identity of the two `LT → LE` literals under the fixed cyclic symmetry;
- non-reachability of the protected target in regime 1;
- reachability of that target after adjoining `AND → OR` in regime 2;
- raw formability of the later rewrite before that reachability expansion.

They do not formalize the empirical search-compression measurements, information-flow experiments, natural-code transfer results, scope/revocation experiments, future-relative comparative representations, or any claim of representation-independent invention or recursive self-extension.

## Palomar statement surface

`Challenge.lean` is self-contained and imports no project-specific source. It defines the finite mathematical objects and states the four advertised theorems in one auditable file. `Solution.lean` supplies the proofs from the project development. `comparator.json` compares exactly those declarations. The package is pinned to Lean 4.32.0 and its CI runs Comparator and NanoDa in addition to ordinary Lean compilation.
